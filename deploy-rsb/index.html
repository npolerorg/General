Timing — Improve First, Then Acquire?

The sequencing question: do we acquire units BEFORE improving the operation (cheaper basis) or AFTER (proven asset)?

OPTION A — ACQUIRE FIRST

Lock units at today's market basis

$120-130K per unit reflects today's actuals (−$800 NOI). Once we visibly improve operations and bylaws, sellers reprice their expectations and the basis runs up.

ADVANTAGES
  • Captures all NOI upside on acquired units
  • 100% of the upside
  • No fiduciary risk
  • Reduces sellers' bargaining power
RISK
Capital deployed before model is proven. If operations don't lift, we own more of a losing portfolio.
OPTION B — IMPROVE FIRST

Prove the new economics, then acquire

De-risk by demonstrating $5K-$10K NOI/unit before deploying acquisition capital. Track record makes financing easier.

ADVANTAGES
  • Capital risk reduced — proven before scaling
  • Time to negotiate special assessment terms
RISK
Visible improvements may push acquisition prices up 20-30%. We pay more, but on proven NOI.
RECOMMENDATION: ACQUIRE COMMERCIAL UNITS FIRST — Move to residential units — Then decide what to do.

WHAT'S ACTUALLY IN FRONT OF US NOW [expand]

The expanded scope

  • Board control across 46-unit association (achieved May 18)
  • Special assessment execution ($1.5M – $4M capital deployment)
  • City extension negotiation and permitting
  • Engineering / contractor / construction oversight (140+ working days minimum)
  • Operator selection and negotiation (SLH, Driftwood, or alternatives)
  • Bylaws and Declaration review and amendment
  • Operational vendor rebids (management, cleaning, security, maintenance)

  • Capital partner relations (if external investors brought in)
  • Potential acquisition of 18-unit Lacomte block
  • Potential acquisition of 9 remaining residential units (Phase 3)
  • Potential acquisition of 4 commercial units (Judith's CU1 + others)
At this scale, who watches the watchers?
A $1M project requires owner attention. A $2M project requires owner supervision. A $4M project — combined with active acquisitions, board governance, contractor oversight, operator selection, and capital partner coordination — requires a dedicated asset management function. The owners' time is best spent on capital decisions, strategic direction, and partner relations. The day-to-day execution of a project of this size and complexity is its own full-time discipline.
WHAT INSTITUTIONAL OWNERS DO
  • Real estate investment trusts and family offices universally separate ownership from asset management at this scale
  • A single point of accountability across construction, operations, and partner coordination prevents value leakage
  • Independent oversight protects owners from the conflicts that arise when a property manager, an engineer, a contractor, and an operator each pursue their own interests
  • Typical asset management fee structures: 1-2% of asset value annually, or fixed monthly retainer plus performance fee
This is a decision the ownership group should make deliberately, with eyes open to the scope of what has been undertaken.
